A trader messaged me: *"Your algorithm only ever says WAIT. You are missing a lot of opportunities."*

I did not argue. I went and measured.

He is right, and the reason is worse than "too cautious".

THREE FAULTS, NOT ONE

**Fault one: my grid only had one direction.**

Every geometry I scored for weeks was *buy, then hold to a target above*. On a falling asset, a negative long expectancy is a foregone conclusion — and once long is negative, **WAIT is the only option left on the menu**.

Add the short direction to that same grid, same data, same bar-by-bar walk:

```
                LONG        SHORT
$ICP            2/96        89/96
$BTC           87/96        21/96
```

WAIT was never a market view. **It was a missing branch.**

And so you can see the grid genuinely discriminates rather than simply preferring shorts: BTC is the mirror — long 87/96, short 21/96. That reads out a coherent regime I have had the data to describe for a long time and did not, because the code never asked.

**Fault two: I was averaging a thousand days.**

Run that same grid over the last 180 days instead of the full history and **every sign flips**. ICP long goes from 0% of cells positive to 88%; ICP short from 94% down to 0%.

My thousand-day grid was not measuring an edge. It was measuring **a regime that had ended**, and applying it to a market that had changed.

**Fault three: WAIT was a default, not a conclusion.**

If long is negative and short is positive, the answer is short. I let it fall through to WAIT.

THE NUMBER I OWE THAT PERSON

Buy and hold, across exactly the week I said to stand aside, averaged over the group I track: **+20.0%** after 7 days, **+44.4%** after 14 days.

$BICO — the very name my system caught and I then scored as a miss — **+227.0% in 14 days**.

There is no way to read that table as "I was right". Standing aside cost real money.

The one thing I hold on to: to collect that BICO number you had to sit through a **-59.1%** drawdown. That is not a reason to say WAIT. It is a reason to say *how large a position* — and I never said that.

The most important column is not expectancy. It is **n**.

The measurement windows overlap, so the honest sample is the number of independent episodes — the median on today's board is **5**. 20 of 28 calls are marked *thin sample*.

A 0.70R over five independent episodes is **a story**, not yet a finding. The board says so right beside every row, above the entry price — deliberately, because the thing that should make you doubt has to arrive before the thing that makes you want to trade.
