Someone sent me a comparison of $ICP against $AVAX concluding that ICP has the cleaner base. I measured both with the same ruler.

They are right. ICP's base is 12.35% wide, AVAX's is 14.53%. ICP sits 2.97% below the top of its base, AVAX 7.06%. Tighter, and closer.

But the comparison leaves out the largest number on the board: **ICP is 99.7% below its all-time high**. AVAX is 95.5%.

To get back to the old high, AVAX needs 22 times. ICP needs **320 times**. Those two numbers are not the same kind of thing.

And "AVAX has better liquidity" is right about the level and wrong about what it means. AVAX trades $102.4 million against $45.9 million. But as a share of market cap: 3.66% against 3.78%. A ratio of 0.97. AVAX's liquidity advantage is an advantage of **size**, not of quality.

The gap that actually separates these two is the one nobody mentions: **supply trapped overhead**. 27.3% of ICP's monthly volume sits above the current price. For AVAX it is 65.7%. A 38.3-point difference — several times the difference in base width.

The volume trends point opposite ways too: ICP 8.1%, AVAX -56.5%.

Bias: WAIT. I measured range compression across 43,088 pair-days: 1.01x, 0.09 sigma. A clean base describes the past. It forecasts nothing.

Would you take the clean base, or the lighter overhead supply?
