Someone sent me an AI-written $ICP plan and asked whether it was worth taking. I re-measured all of it. Two of its market descriptions are backwards — but that is not what kills this trade.

What kills it is a piece of advice almost every account repeats: **wait for confirmation before you enter.**

THE TWO WRONG SENTENCES FIRST

The plan says price is "near the bottom of the range, around 2.17–2.20".

ICP is at 2.22. The 30-day range runs from 1.994 to 2.290. The true position: **75.7%**. That is near the top of the month, not the bottom.

On a 90-day window it genuinely is low — 16.8%. But "near the bottom of the range" without naming the window is a sentence that is true at one lookback and false at the other two.

Second sentence: "volume is still weak, no clear expansion yet".

The last three days are running **69.7% higher** than the ten before them. Volume z-score 1.15. Volume is expanding, not weak.

One thing they got right: correlation with BTC is "moderate". I measure r = 0.564 over 30 days. Genuinely moderate.

NOW THE PART THAT MATTERS

The plan reads: *only go long on a clear close above 2.30–2.35, stop below 2.10–2.13, targets 2.50 → 2.80 → 3.30.*

Read one line at a time, it sounds reasonable. Put together, it breaks.

Because **the stop does not move with the entry.** You wait for a higher price to get in, but the stop stays where it was. The risk leg gets longer and the reward leg gets shorter.

Same 2.12 stop, two ways in:

```
                 ENTER NOW  WAIT FOR CONFIRMATION
entry                 2.22                    2.33
risk                 4.64%                   9.03%
TP1 2.50          +12.71%                  +7.53%
R:R                   2.74                    0.83
win rate needed      26.8%                   54.5%
```

The last row is the one to look at. Enter now: you need to be right 26.8% of the time to break even — you can be wrong most of the time and survive. Wait for confirmation: you must be **right more than half the time** just to break even.

Waiting did not make the trade safer. It moved the trade from "wrong often and still alive" to "must be right regularly".

SO — IS IT RIGHT REGULARLY?

This is where measuring beats guessing.

I walked back through 1,000 days of ICP history, **bar by bar**. From each day, does price reach the target first or the stop first? A bar that reaches both on the same day is charged to the stop — inside a daily candle nobody knows the true order, and a plan should not get the benefit of that ambiguity.

For the "wait for confirmation" geometry (+7.53% before −9.03%), at a 30-day horizon:

```
target first    49.2%
stop first      50.7%
                970 windows
```

49.2% against the 54.5% required.

The rest of the target ladder is thinner still: 2.80 before the stop 27.5% of the time, 3.30 before the stop 12.0%.

Expectancy for the whole plan: **-0.10R.** Negative.

And to be fair — entering now does not save it either. Reaching +12.71% before −4.64% happens just 23.0% of the time, expectancy -0.14R. Both are negative. I am not defending either one. I am saying which is worse, and why.

WOULD THAT STOP SURVIVE A WEEK?

104 completed weeks of ICP, high-to-low range within the week:

```
p25       13.92%
median    19.60%
p90       42.35%
```

A 9.03% stop is **0.46 times** a median week. Hold through a week and a completely ordinary week takes it out, with no news required.

THREE THINGS I COULD NOT CHECK

Saying plainly where I am blind, because that is the part most analysis hides.

**One.** Binance funding and open interest: the futures endpoint is geo-blocked from this host. No number, so I say nothing.

**Two.** Real liquidation data: CoinGlass, Glassnode and CryptoQuant all refuse without a paid API. No free data at this time.

**Three.** Conditioning on "only days that break a 30-day high" leaves 28 samples, which is about 0.9 independent episodes once you remove the overlap. Far too few to conclude anything — including the conclusion that it is useless.

THE TAKEAWAY, WHICH HAS NOTHING TO DO WITH ICP

Any time you read a plan shaped like *"enter above X, stop below Y"*, do exactly one calculation before anything else:

**The distance from X down to Y, against the distance from X up to the first target.**

If the lower leg is longer than the upper leg, that plan needs you to be right regularly — and almost no plan tells you what "regularly" means. Here it is 54.5%.

That number takes ten seconds to compute. It filters out a great deal.

Bias: **WAIT**. Not because ICP is bad — the 25-day base is 12.35% wide, volume genuinely is expanding, the structure is clean. But that specific price ladder, with that specific stop, does not pay for the risk it asks you to take. And on range compression I have already measured 43,088 pair-days: 1.01x, 0.09 sigma — a pretty base describes the past, it does not forecast the future.

One last note on the correlation figure, because it is routinely misread: r = 0.56 does **not** mean BTC drives more than half the movement. You have to square it — $BTC explains roughly **31.8%** of ICP's daily variance. The other two thirds is ICP's own business. But a third is more than enough for one bad BTC session to erase everything in the table above.

When did you last compute the win rate a trade requires before taking it?
