Someone sent me a technical analysis of the viral story about a 19-year-old turning $68 into $750,000 with an arbitrage bot. The analysis concluded it was not feasible.

The conclusion is right. But it argues in words — latency is too high, fees eat it, HFT is faster. All true sentences, and all uncheckable.

I measured it. On exactly the kind of machine being discussed.

HOW IT WAS MEASURED

Two venues: Binance spot and OKX spot. Eight pairs. 40 samples, 3 seconds apart, **both venues polled in the same beat** — sample them a few seconds apart and the "spread" you find is just time passing, not an opportunity.

320 observations in total.

And most importantly: I measured **executable prices**, not the midpoint. You buy at the ask and sell at the bid. The midpoint is a price nobody trades at — and it is exactly where every "arbitrage opportunity" table gets its artificial beauty.

REAL LATENCY FROM THIS MACHINE

```
Binance                       160 ms      p90 172 ms
OKX                           193 ms      p90 232 ms
```

The analysis says retail bots run at 50–300ms. **Correct.** My numbers sit inside that. A point to them.

REAL PRICE SPREADS

```
                              median         maximum
executable                   0.0000%         0.0203%
round-trip fees                0.20%
```

Read those two lines again.

The largest gap that appeared across 320 observations is **0.0203%**. The fee for both legs is **0.20%**.

The fee is **9.8 times** larger than the best opportunity.

THE NUMBER THAT ANSWERS EVERYTHING

```
positive gap exists            23.4%       before fees
actually profitable             0.0%        after fees
```

Gaps **do** open — 23.4% of the time the two venues genuinely disagree. That is the part that makes people believe.

Times the gap was wide enough to pay the fee: **not once.** Not rare. Zero out of 320.

The best round trip I observed still loses **0.180%**.

AND HERE IS WHERE THE STORY COLLAPSES

$68 to $750,000 is **11,029 times**.

Assume the impossible: every trade wins, none loses, all profit compounds, nothing is withdrawn.

```
consecutive winning trades    spread needed    vs the maximum
100                                 9.955%              490x
1,000                               1.135%               56x
10,000                              0.293%               14x
100,000                             0.209%               10x
```

The more trades you run, the less each one needs. But it never drops below **0.20%**, because that is the fee — you pay it before there is any profit at all.

Which means: **no number of trades makes this story work.** Not hard. Non-existent. Every round makes the account smaller, and a series multiplied by a number below 1 never grows 11,029 times.

That is why I would rather measure than argue. You can argue about latency. You cannot argue with multiplication.

TWO PLACES THE OTHER ANALYSIS WAS OFF

They are right in the conclusion. But two details are wrong, and both are wrong in the direction of being **too generous to the story**.

**One.** They describe arbitrage spreads as sitting in a 0.05–0.30% band. I measured a median of **0.0000%** — meaning that most of the time there is no executable gap at all, rather than a small one. The largest gap in all 320 observations barely reaches the **bottom** of their band and falls far short of its top. The market is far tighter than even the person debunking it assumed.

**Two.** They worried that $68 of capital would not clear minimum order sizes. I checked: the Binance minimum on these pairs is $5. That capital clears it comfortably. The real barrier is not order size — it is the fee, and no amount of capital escapes that.

As for "scanning 50+ markets is a bandwidth problem"? I pulled all eight pairs with **one** request per venue. Scanning is not expensive. Scanning and finding nothing is the problem.

WHAT I COULD NOT CHECK

I sampled prices over REST every 3 seconds. A real bot uses WebSocket and sees more ticks — so **I cannot measure how long a gap survives**, and I cannot rule out brief gaps slipping between my samples.

But that bot still pays the same 0.20% and still looks at these same two order books. Seeing more ticks does not make the fee smaller.

CEX–DEX arbitrage, prediction markets and gas costs: **no free data at this time**, so I say nothing about them.

WHAT IS ACTUALLY WORTH REMEMBERING

Arbitrage is real. It is the profession of people who put servers inside the exchange's own data centre, pay the lowest fee tier, and live on a few thousandths of a percent multiplied by enormous volume.

What is not real is the version you can run from a bedroom with $68.

And the way to test a story like this is not to argue about technology. It is to ask: **what spread, what fees, measured over how many samples?** Those three questions take ten minutes to answer with public data, and no viral story survives them.

This post takes no position on any market. It is just a subtraction.

Have you ever subtracted the fees from an opportunity before believing it?
