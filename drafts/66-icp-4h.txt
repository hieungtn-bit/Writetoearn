Someone sent me a 4H analysis of $ICP — support zones, resistance zones, bull and bear scenarios. The kind of post everybody reads every day.

I split it into three layers, because the three layers fail in three different ways.

LAYER ONE: DESCRIBING THE PRESENT

This is just a lookup. It either matches or it does not.

The analysis says price is at 2.18–2.21. The real price when I measured: **2.232**. Already out of that zone, to the upside.

They put 4H RSI at "around 55–65 depending on the session". I measure 66.0. Just over the edge.

What they got **right** is volume: *"no clear expansion yet."* The 4H volume z-score is -0.21, and the last six bars run just 4.7% above the twenty-four before them. Accurate.

What they got **wrong** is the moving average: *"the longer MA is still pressing down from above."* On the 4H, the SMA200 sits at 2.1568. Price at 2.232 is **above** it, not below.

LAYER TWO: THE BOX

They write that ICP is compressing inside 2.08–2.26, 8.65% wide.

I measured the last 60 4H bars — about ten days. The real range: **1.9940 to 2.2570**, 13.19% wide.

And here is the number worth looking at: **33.3% of bars closed OUTSIDE that box.**

One third. A box that a third of bars close outside of is not a box. It is a line drawn around where price happened to spend time.

As for "compression"? The box is 8.65% wide. An ordinary ICP day travels 3.62%. So that entire "long-term compression zone" is **2.39 normal trading days**.

Two and a half days is not stillness. It is two and a half days.

LAYER TWO AND A HALF: COUNTING INSTEAD OF DESCRIBING

"Has held quite well." "Repeatedly rejected." These sound like observations. They are actually **counts**. So I counted.

Within the measurable window (dropping the last 12 bars, which have no outcome yet to score):

```
zone              touches   later higher   broke down
2.15–2.17               0              —            —
2.08–2.12              36             11           20
2.00–2.05               9              9            0
2.22–2.26               0              —            —
2.30–2.35               0              —            —
```

The 2.22–2.26 resistance is described as *"repeatedly rejected"*. Times price touched it in the measurable window: **0**. There are no rejections to repeat — price only reached it in the last few bars, and right now it is standing **inside** that zone.

The 2.15–2.17 zone is described as *"being tested"*. Touches: **0**. Price is at 2.232, well above it.

The zone they call primary support, 2.08–2.12: touched 36 times, and **20 of those were followed by a close below it**. More than half. That is a zone that tends to break, not one that tends to hold.

The only zone that held perfectly is 2.00–2.05: 9 touches, 9 times higher afterwards, 0 breakdowns. And it is the zone they ranked **lowest**.

LAYER THREE: IS SUPPORT REAL AT ALL?

This is the question underneath all the others, and almost nobody puts a number on it.

I defined "a support test" mechanically: price within 1.0% of the low of the prior 20 bars. Then asked: 12 bars later, is price higher or lower? And — the part that matters most — **how does that compare to any random bar?**

A 59% bounce rate says nothing if the market rises 59% of the time anyway.

```
after a support test    59.3%
any bar                 45.7%
difference             +13.6    points
```

**Support beats the baseline.** By 13.6 percentage points, across 135 tests.

I have to say this plainly: this is the first thing in weeks of measuring that has come out **positive**. I measured range compression — nothing. Hour of day — nothing. Intraday direction — nothing. This one has something.

But it is not finished.

Those 135 tests **overlap** — they share the same bars with each other. Treated as independent, the result is **3.17 sigma**, which sounds compelling. Remove the overlap and about 11.3 genuinely independent episodes remain, and the same difference is only **0.92 sigma**.

Below one sigma is nothing proven.

I am leaving both numbers here rather than picking the prettier one, because this is exactly where most backtests die: print the 3.17, omit the 0.92, and call a coin flip an edge.

The honest conclusion: **support appears to carry a little information, and I have not proven it.** Enough for me to keep measuring. Not enough for me to put money on it.

AS FOR THE TRADE THEY PROPOSE

*"Buy the reaction at support 2.12–2.15 with a tight stop below 2.08."*

```
entry              2.135
stop               2.075       2.81%
= daily ATR         0.78       times
```

"Tight" is literally correct: that stop is **0.78 times** one ordinary daily range of ICP. Less than a day.

Walking bar by bar across 970 4H windows:

```
                     hit    stopped out    expectancy
TP 2.26 · 2 days   17.0%          54.5%       -0.190R
TP 2.26 · 5 days   24.9%          69.1%       -0.171R
TP 2.35 · 2 days    8.1%          56.2%       -0.272R
TP 2.35 · 5 days   15.2%          73.5%       -0.192R
```

Four cells, four negative numbers. Reward-to-risk of 2.08 and 3.58 looks fine, but multiplied by the hit rate no cell pays.

And I saw this exact shape yesterday on XLM: a stop under one daily range is close to certain to be swept, regardless of where the target sits. Twice is not a rule — but the mechanism is clear, and it is easy to check: **divide the stop distance by daily ATR.** Below 1 and you are paying for noise.

WHERE I AM BLIND

The zone counting drops the last 12 bars, because they have no outcome to score yet. That is exactly why 2.22–2.26 shows 0 touches even though price is inside it right now. I am saying so rather than leaving you to guess.

Bias: **WAIT**. Price is above the 4H SMA10, SMA20, SMA50 and SMA200 — the short-term structure genuinely does lean up, and the analysis feels that direction correctly. But the specific levels in it do not describe what happened, and the trade attached to them is negative on all four measurements. Feeling the right direction with the wrong geometry is still the wrong plan.

Next time you read a "support and resistance zones" post, try exactly one question: **how many times has price touched that zone, and how many times did it hold?** That question turns a picture into a count, and counts are not arguable.

Have you ever tried counting?
